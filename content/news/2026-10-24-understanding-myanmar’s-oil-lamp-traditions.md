🪔 UNDERSTANDING MYANMAR’S OIL-LAMP TRADITIONS

The Cultural Origins and Significance of the Rakhine Buddha Pujaniya Oil-Lamp Dance



Cultural traditions are more than beautiful performances or meaningful ceremonies. They carry history, religious devotion, artistic expression, and the identity of the communities that have preserved them across generations.



Recently, discussions surrounding Myanmar’s oil-lamp traditions have led to disagreements among people identifying with different ethnic backgrounds, particularly Rakhine and Bamar communities. Rather than allowing these differences to become a source of division, this is an opportunity to learn about the origins and meanings of these traditions with respect and understanding.



🪔 1. Thadingyut: The Tradition of Lighting Oil Lamps



Thadingyut, Myanmar’s Festival of Lights, is associated with the Buddhist tradition commemorating the Buddha’s return from Tāvatiṃsa Heaven. During this period, people light oil lamps and candles as expressions of religious devotion. Paying respect to parents, teachers, elders, and other respected individuals is also an important part of the festival.



Lighting lamps during Thadingyut is a tradition observed in many parts of Myanmar. It reflects Buddhist faith, gratitude, reverence, and the values passed down through generations.



However, the broader tradition of lighting lamps should not be confused with every specific form of oil-lamp performance. Different communities may have their own distinctive artistic expressions, histories, and ceremonial practices.



🪔 2. The Origins and Significance of the Rakhine Buddha Pujaniya Oil-Lamp Dance



The Rakhine Buddha Pujaniya oil-lamp dance is a distinctive cultural and devotional art form associated with Rakhine traditions.



According to a report published by Myanmar Digital News, the Buddha Pujaniya song was composed by U Batha in 1965. The song and its associated performance are connected with paying homage to the Triple Gem: the Buddha, the Dhamma, and the Sangha.



Through its combination of music, dance, and illuminated lamps, this tradition expresses religious reverence while preserving elements of Rakhine artistic and cultural identity.



It is therefore important to understand the Rakhine Buddha Pujaniya oil-lamp dance within its own cultural context. Recognizing its Rakhine heritage is not an act of excluding other communities; it is an act of acknowledging the history and identity associated with a particular tradition.



🪔 3. Understanding the Difference Without Creating Division



The general practice of lighting oil lamps during Thadingyut and the Rakhine Buddha Pujaniya oil-lamp dance are related through the use of lamps and Buddhist devotion, but they are not necessarily the same tradition.



Thadingyut lamp-lighting customs are widely observed across Myanmar, while the Buddha Pujaniya oil-lamp dance has a distinct connection to Rakhine cultural and artistic heritage.



Understanding this distinction matters because recognizing the cultural origins of a tradition does not mean claiming that other communities have no place in appreciating it.



A tradition can be appreciated by people from different ethnic backgrounds while its origins are still acknowledged accurately. Cultural appreciation and respect for cultural identity can—and should—exist together.



🪔 4. A Shared Responsibility to Preserve Myanmar’s Cultural Heritage



Myanmar is home to diverse ethnic communities, each with its own history, traditions, and artistic contributions. These differences are part of the country’s cultural richness.



Whether we are Rakhine, Bamar, or members of other ethnic communities, we can all contribute to preserving Myanmar’s cultural heritage by learning about traditions accurately, acknowledging their origins, and treating one another with respect.



Preserving culture does not mean arguing over which community deserves greater recognition. It means ensuring that the meaning, history, and identity of each tradition are understood and passed on to future generations.



When discussing cultural practices, we should rely on credible information rather than assumptions, personal attacks, or ethnic stereotypes. Disagreement should be an opportunity to learn—not a reason to insult or divide one another.



Loving our own culture does not require us to diminish another’s. Recognizing one community’s heritage does not erase the value of another’s.

We are sharing this article not to take part in ethnic disputes, but to encourage a better understanding of Myanmar’s cultural traditions and the importance of preserving them with accuracy, dignity, and mutual respect.



We may come from different communities and inherit different traditions, but we can share a common responsibility: to protect cultural heritage, respect its origins, and pass its meaning on to future generations.



Let us choose understanding over division, respect over hostility, and cultural preservation over conflict.
